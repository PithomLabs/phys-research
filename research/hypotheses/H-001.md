# WEP prediction for the MICROSCOPE Ti/Pt pair

# source: Touboul et al., "MICROSCOPE mission: final results of the test of
# the Equivalence Principle", Phys. Rev. Lett. 129, 121102 (2022),
# arXiv:2209.15487v1, recorded as P-001. What follows is the pseudocode
# author's arrangement of what P-001 states, derives and cites; every
# attribution note is ordinary prose (PSEUDO.md §8), never syntax.

given published_estimate: eta_tipt = [−1.5 ± 2.3 (stat) ± 1.5 (syst)] × 10⁻¹⁵ at 1σ statistical, with the systematic term reported as an upper bound, from the MICROSCOPE final data set; P-001 states that the approximated ratio delta_tipt is identified with eta_tipt in this result (P-001, Eq. (5))
define eta_tipt := η(Ti,Pt), the Eötvös parameter for the titanium/platinum test-mass pair, written (a_Ti − a_Pt)/(a_Ti + a_Pt)
define delta_tipt := δ(Ti,Pt), the approximated Eötvös ratio the instrument actually estimates, written (mg/mi)_Ti − (mg/mi)_Pt (P-001, Eq. (2))
define sigma_total := a combined uncertainty for eta_tipt, formed from the published statistical and systematic terms by a rule declared before evaluation
assume budget_adequacy: the systematic term of published_estimate bounds the unmodelled thermal, magnetic and gravitational-gradient effects at the EP frequency for this data set
claim wep_prediction: under WEP, eta_tipt = 0
derive consistency: |eta_tipt| ≤ sigma_total from [published_estimate, eta_tipt, sigma_total, budget_adequacy] | taking sigma_total as the quadrature sum of 2.3 and 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵, about 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁵, the central value 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ sits near 0.5σ, so under that declared prescription the estimate does not separate eta_tipt from zero
if |eta_tipt| > sigma_total:
    then the published estimate would falsify wep_prediction for the Ti/Pt pair in this regime
    else the published estimate would not falsify wep_prediction for the Ti/Pt pair in this regime
? which total-uncertainty prescription for sigma_total is declared before evaluation, and does the verdict on wep_prediction change between quadrature and linear combination of the published statistical and systematic terms?
? is the systematic term of published_estimate bounded independently of the same fit that produced it, and would a bound from held-out sessions, or a measured differential thermal and magnetic coupling between the Ti/Al/V and Pt/Rh masses at the EP frequency, close this?

## Falsification condition

The WEP prediction for the Ti/Pt pair, eta(Ti,Pt) = 0, would be falsified in
the tested regime by a measurement or re-analysis of the MICROSCOPE data
giving |eta(Ti,Pt)| greater than a declared number of standard deviations of
a total uncertainty that is **declared before the value is evaluated**.

The paper publishes the two uncertainty terms separately — statistical
2.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ (1σ) and systematic upper bound 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ for SUEP — and
publishes no combined total. Any total used in a falsification test is
therefore a declared prescription, not a paper result. Combining the two in
quadrature gives approximately 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁵; combining them linearly gives
3.8 × 10⁻¹⁵. Whichever is chosen must be written down, with its
justification, before the comparison is made — choosing it afterwards would
make the test unfalsifiable in practice.

The regime is the 2016–2018 MICROSCOPE data set, the Ti/Al/V versus Pt/Rh
composition pair, and the systematic budget actually achieved by that
instrument. The condition says nothing about other composition pairs, other
missions, or composition-independent tests of the WEP.

Falsifying the WEP prediction for this pair would be evidence against the
WEP in this regime; failing to falsify it does **not** establish the WEP —
the result remains an upper bound consistent with the prediction. The
unresolved questions recorded above bear directly on how much weight that
upper bound can carry.

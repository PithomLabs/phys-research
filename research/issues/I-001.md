# Does the MICROSCOPE 2022 result confirm the WEP for the Ti/Pt pair?

## Question

The MICROSCOPE final result constrains the Eötvös parameter for the
titanium/platinum pair to η(Ti, Pt) = [−1.5 ± 2.3 (stat) ± 1.5 (syst)] ×
10⁻¹⁵. The open problem is what this number does and does not establish:
whether the Weak Equivalence Principle is confirmed for this composition
pair, whether the result is instead only an upper bound consistent with the
WEP, and what would be required to move from "consistent with" to
"confirmed" — in particular whether the statistical and systematic
uncertainties can be reduced far enough, and under which declared
total-uncertainty prescription, for any such statement to be meaningful.

## Theoretical expectation

- Claim or prediction: Under the Weak Equivalence Principle, inertial and gravitational mass are equal for all bodies, so the Eötvös parameter for any composition pair vanishes: η(Ti, Pt) = 0 exactly.
- Assumptions: Local Lorentz invariance and local position invariance; no composition-dependent long-range fifth force coupling differently to Ti and Pt in the tested regime; the standard model of the instrument's systematic effects is adequate.
- Applicable regime: Solar-system gravity, weak-field Earth orbit, the Ti/Al/V versus Pt/Rh composition pair, over the 2016–2018 mission data set.
- Source or derivation reference: P-001, Eq. (1) and Eq. (2) for the definition of η and its approximation δ; P-001, abstract and Eq. (5) for the measured constraint.

## Independent theoretical result

NOT APPLICABLE — no independent theoretical derivation is required for this experimental result.

## Experimental expectation

- Predicted observable: η(Ti, Pt), the Eötvös parameter for the Ti/Pt test-mass pair, estimated from the differential acceleration of two collocated free-falling masses of different composition.
- Units and conditions: Dimensionless. Drag-free satellite in sun-synchronous dawn-dusk orbit, test masses controlled by electrostatic forces, measurement along the longitudinal sensitive x axis, EP signal modulated at the spin-plus-orbital frequency fEP.
- Uncertainty: Statistical uncertainty 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ (1σ) and systematic upper bound 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵, recorded separately as published. The paper does not publish a combined total uncertainty; any figure combining them is a declared prescription, not a paper result.
- Falsification condition: A measurement or re-analysis yielding η(Ti, Pt) significantly different from zero under a total-uncertainty prescription declared before evaluation would count against the WEP prediction for this pair.

## Experimental actual

- Measured result: η(Ti, Pt) = [−1.5 ± 2.3 (stat) ± 1.5 (syst)] × 10⁻¹⁵. The paper states that it found no violation of the WEP. The reference instrument gave η(Pt, Pt) = [0.0 ± 1.1 (stat) ± 2.3 (syst)] × 10⁻¹⁵ as a null check.
- Units and uncertainty: Dimensionless; statistical error at 1σ (2.3 × 10⁻¹⁵); systematic upper bound 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ for SUEP (2.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ for SUREF) against a specification of 0.2 × 10⁻¹⁵. Stat and syst are kept separate as published.
- Methodology and experimental conditions: Eighteen SUEP and nine SUREF sessions with all data calibrated; Adam frequency-domain estimation cross-checked against M-ECM; glitches masked by recursive σ-clipping and treated as missing data; thermal systematics characterized by dedicated heater and tilt sessions, with temperature variations during EP sessions below the probe noise at fEP so that only a pessimistic upper bound was available.
- Exact source locator: Eq. (5) and Fig. 1 of P-001 (p. 5), with the systematic discussion on p. 4 and the thermal discussion on p. 5; per-segment estimates in Fig. 1, off-centerings in Table III.

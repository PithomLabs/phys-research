## Finding details

### F1 — Falsification threshold is a free parameter

- Where: `## Falsification condition` first sentence (H-001 lines 36–39) and `if |eta_tipt| > sigma_total:` (lines 28–30).
- What: Under-specified falsification condition / free threshold. The required section says "a declared number of standard deviations" but never declares the number; the pseudo silently uses one σ_total. The outcome is sensitive to this undeclared multiplier.
- Why: The published central value is 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵. With quadrature σ_total ≈ 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁵, N = 1 does not falsify (1.5 < 2.7), but N = 0.5 does (1.5 > 1.37); with the linear total 3.8 × 10⁻¹⁵, N = 0.5 does not (1.5 < 1.9). The record itself argues that choosing the prescription after seeing the value would make the test unfalsifiable in practice; leaving N free has the same effect. The `?` at line 31 asks about the combination rule but not about N, and the `if` does not state N.
- Minimum fix: Declare N explicitly (e.g., "1 standard deviation") and the combination rule in the `## Falsification condition` section before the comparison, and make the pseudo's `if` use the same N. If the intent is a schema, state what fixes N and why it cannot be chosen after the data.

### F2 — `eta_tipt` conflates the parameter and the published estimate

- Where: `define eta_tipt` (line 22) versus `given published_estimate` (line 21); `derive consistency` (line 27); `if |eta_tipt| > sigma_total` (line 28); and the prose "a measurement ... giving |eta(Ti,Pt)|" (lines 37–38).
- What: Role/quantity confusion (parameter versus estimator). One name denotes both the exact Eötvös parameter and the published measured interval.
- Why: The paper's Eq. (5) reports an estimate of η, not η itself. A parameter is a fixed unknown; an uncertainty is a property of the estimator. The `given` assigns a measured interval to the parameter; the derive's result is about the parameter while its reasoning is about the central estimate; and the `if` compares the parameter to an uncertainty. If `eta_tipt` is the parameter, `|eta_tipt| > sigma_total` is not evaluable from the published estimate, and under the WEP prediction η = 0 it is false by construction, so the conditional cannot test the prediction. The branch text ("the published estimate would falsify") shows that the intended subject is the estimate.
- Minimum fix: Introduce a separate name for the estimate (e.g., `eta_tipt_hat`, or use `published_estimate` as the estimate) and reserve `eta_tipt` for the parameter; restate the derive as a statement about the estimate's distance from zero and the `if` as `|eta_tipt_hat| > sigma_total`; in the prose say "a measurement whose estimate of eta ..." rather than "giving |eta|".

### F3 — `derive consistency` converts consistency into a parameter bound

- Where: `derive consistency: |eta_tipt| ≤ sigma_total from [...]` (line 27).
- What: Invalid inference / overreach: consistency with zero is converted into a bound on the true parameter; the coverage premise is missing.
- Why: The premises give a central estimate and two uncertainty terms; they do not give a coverage probability for the combined interval. "The estimate is 0.5σ from zero" means the estimate is consistent with zero, not that the true parameter satisfies |η| ≤ σ_total. On the natural Gaussian reading of the quoted 1σ statistical error alone, the 1σ interval around −1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ extends to about −3.8 × 10⁻¹⁵ (adding the systematic term widens it further), so values with |η| > 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁵ are not excluded at 1σ; the derive's result is stronger than the data. The paper reports a 68% confidence region around the estimate (Fig. 1), not a guarantee that |η| ≤ 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁵. (This is the inference defect; F2 is the symbol defect.)
- Minimum fix: Restate the result as "the published estimate is within 1σ_total of zero under the declared combination rule", or add an explicit coverage premise and state the confidence level; do not assert a bound on the parameter without it.

### F4 — Quadrature treats the systematic upper bound as a 1σ standard deviation

- Where: `derive consistency` reasoning (line 27), `define sigma_total` (line 24), and `## Falsification condition` second paragraph (lines 41–48).
- What: Statistical/systematic conflation: the 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ published systematic upper bound is combined in quadrature with the 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ 1σ statistical error as if the bound were a standard deviation, and the result is called a total uncertainty / σ.
- Why: The paper reports 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ as a 1σ statistical error and 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ as a systematic upper bound for SUEP; the thermal term is explicitly a pessimistic upper bound because temperature variations were below the probe noise at fEP. A quadrature sum presupposes independent standard deviations; an upper bound has no standard deviation and no stated coverage. The 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁵ figure is a declared convention, not a 1σ total, and "0.5σ" has no coverage. The prose acknowledges the upper-bound status, but the derive still treats the term as a σ; the `?` at line 32 asks about independence, not about distribution. Quadrature also assumes statistical/systematic independence, which is not declared.
- Minimum fix: State the conversion/coverage convention explicitly (e.g., treat the systematic as a conservative additive bound, giving 3.8 × 10⁻¹⁵, or report the result as a bound rather than a σ) and add a `?` or premise for the missing characterization; do not call the quadrature sum a 1σ total without justification.

### F5 — `budget_adequacy` overreaches and is unused

- Where: `assume budget_adequacy` (line 25) and the `derive consistency` premise list (line 27).
- What: Unsupported assumption / source overreach, plus an unnecessary premise.
- Why: The paper reports an upper bound on characterized systematics, not a bound on unmodelled effects. It says the overall systematics upper bound is 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ for SUEP and that all contributors except non-linearity and temperature variations are below requirements; the thermal term is characterized by dedicated sessions and is a pessimistic upper bound. A bound on known effects does not bound unknown ones. The derive lists `budget_adequacy` but its reasoning does not use it; if it is meant to license the systematic term, it is doing load-bearing work the source cannot support.
- Minimum fix: Weaken the assumption to the reported bound on characterized thermal, magnetic and gravity-gradient terms, add a separate `?` for unmodelled effects, and either use the assumption explicitly in the derivation or remove it from the premise list.

### F6 — `define eta_tipt` omits the factor 2 in Eq. (1)

- Where: `define eta_tipt` (line 22).
- What: Definition inconsistent with the cited source (source-fidelity defect).
- Why: The paper's Eq. (1) as printed is η_{A,B} = 2(a_A − a_B)/(a_A + a_B). H-001 omits the factor 2, so its η is approximately δ/2, contradicting the `given`'s statement that the paper identifies δ with η. With the factor 2, η ≈ δ; without it, the identification fails. The Eq. (5) value is quoted independently, so the falsification arithmetic is not changed, but the definition is not the quantity the paper defines. P-001's claim has the same omission, so the error is inherited; H-001 is still the review target.
- Minimum fix: Restore the factor 2 in the definition, or state the convention explicitly and reconcile it with the δ ≈ η identification; correct the source record's transcription if appropriate.

### F7 — `define sigma_total` is not a definition and the rule is absent from the premises

- Where: `define sigma_total` (line 24) and the `derive consistency` premise list (line 27).
- What: Role confusion / omitted premise. The `define` defers the combination rule ("by a rule declared before evaluation") instead of specifying a mathematical object; the rule appears only in the derive's reasoning ("taking sigma_total as the quadrature sum") and is not in the premise list.
- Why: PSEUDO §3's substitution test: replacing `sigma_total` by its RHS gives "a combined uncertainty formed by a rule declared before evaluation", which does not determine a value; the derive's numerical claim depends on the quadrature rule. PSEUDO §2.6 requires an essential quantity to be introduced earlier and listed when it carries the step. The `?` at line 31 acknowledges the gap but does not fix the derive's use of an undeclared rule.
- Minimum fix: Make the rule explicit in the `define` (e.g., `define sigma_total := sqrt(stat² + syst²)`, with the terms named) or introduce it as an `assume`/`given` and list it in the derive; alternatively, state the result conditionally on the rule and put the rule in the premise list.

### F8 — The prescription `?` and the derive disagree about what is declared

- Where: `?` line 31 versus `derive consistency` line 27.
- What: Internal inconsistency / `?` content defect. The `?` asks "which total-uncertainty prescription for sigma_total is declared before evaluation", while the derive says "under that declared prescription" and uses quadrature. The record both defers and asserts the prescription.
- Why: If quadrature is the declared prescription, the `?` should be resolved or narrowed to "is quadrature the right choice?"; if it is not declared, the derive's "under that declared prescription" is unsupported. The `?` cannot be both an open question and a premise of the derive. The first `?` also does not mention the threshold N, which is the more consequential free parameter (F1).
- Minimum fix: Declare the prescription in the `define` and reword the `?` to ask whether the verdict is robust to the alternative combination, or remove "declared" from the derive and mark the result as conditional on the choice.

### F9 — Forward reference in `given published_estimate` (minor structural)

- Where: `given published_estimate` (line 21) uses `eta_tipt` and `delta_tipt` before the `define`s on lines 22–23.
- What: Declaration-before-use / forward reference under PSEUDO §2.1 (if those tokens are read as the PSEUDO names). Minor structural, not a premise-list failure.
- Why: The names are introduced later; the `given` content uses them earlier. The rule's strict clause is about premise lists, and all actual premise-list names are introduced earlier, so this is not a premise-list error; but "No forward references" and the later premise lists treat these as names. The `given`'s attribution sentence uses `delta_tipt` before its definition.
- Minimum fix: Move the `define eta_tipt` and `define delta_tipt` lines above the `given`, or rewrite the `given` to use notation that is not claimed as a PSEUDO name until after the definitions.

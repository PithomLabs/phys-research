## Finding details

### F1 — Falsification threshold is an undeclared free parameter

- Where: `## Falsification condition`, first sentence (H-001 lines 36–39), and `if |eta_tipt| > sigma_total:` (lines 28–30).
- What: Under-specified falsification condition: the threshold multiplier is a free parameter. The section requires "a declared number of standard deviations" but never declares the number; the pseudo's conditional compares `|eta_tipt|` directly to `sigma_total`, silently selecting the multiplier implicit in that comparison.
- Why: A condition whose threshold is not fixed can be satisfied or defeated by choosing the multiplier after seeing the estimate. The record itself says that choosing the prescription after seeing the value "would make the test unfalsifiable in practice" (lines 46–48); the same objection applies to the multiplier, which is not mentioned in either `?` line. The pinned I-001 repeats the same under-specification ("significantly different from zero ... under a total-uncertainty prescription declared before evaluation"), so the input chain does not supply the missing multiplier either. The sensitivity is not hypothetical: the central value is 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵, while the record's two candidate totals are of the same order (≈ 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁵ and 3.8 × 10⁻¹⁵), so the comparison's outcome changes across a plausible range of multipliers. This is blocking because a falsification condition with a free threshold cannot in principle fail for a suitable choice and therefore does not yet state a test.
- Minimum fix: Declare the multiplier explicitly in the `## Falsification condition` before any comparison, make the pseudo's `if` use the same value, and either add a `?` or a sentence explaining what fixes it independently of the observed estimate. I do not propose a value.

### F2 — `eta_tipt` conflates the exact parameter with the published estimate

- Where: `define eta_tipt` (line 22) versus `given published_estimate` (line 21); `derive consistency` (line 27); `if |eta_tipt| > sigma_total` (line 28); and the prose "a measurement ... giving |eta(Ti,Pt)|" (lines 37–38).
- What: Role/quantity confusion: one name denotes both the exact Eötvös parameter (a fixed unknown) and the published measured interval (an estimator with an uncertainty).
- Why: P-001 Eq. (5) reports an estimate of η, not η itself; an uncertainty is a property of the estimator, not of the parameter. The `given` assigns a measured interval to the parameter; the `derive`'s stated result is about the parameter while its reasoning is about the central estimate; and the `if` compares the parameter to an uncertainty. If `eta_tipt` is the parameter, then under `claim wep_prediction` it equals zero by construction, so `|eta_tipt| > sigma_total` is false without reference to any data and the conditional cannot test the prediction. The branch text ("the published estimate would falsify") and the prose ("a measurement ... giving |η|") show that the intended subject is the estimate. This is blocking independently of F1: even a fixed multiplier would not make the conditional test the prediction while the name is ambiguous.
- Minimum fix: Introduce a separate name for the estimate (for example `eta_tipt_hat`) and reserve `eta_tipt` for the parameter; restate the derive and the `if` in terms of the estimate; change the prose to "a measurement whose estimate of η ...". Do not resolve the WEP claim in the process.

### F3 — `derive consistency` converts consistency into a bound on the parameter

- Where: `derive consistency: |eta_tipt| ≤ sigma_total from [...]` (line 27).
- What: Invalid inference / overreach: consistency of an estimate with zero is converted into a bound on the true parameter; a coverage premise is missing.
- Why: The premises give a central estimate, two uncertainty terms, and a declared combination; they do not give a coverage probability for the combined interval. The derive's reasoning shows only that the estimate is close to zero relative to the declared combination, not that the parameter satisfies |η| ≤ sigma_total. P-001's Fig. 1 gives a confidence region, not a hard bound, and an interval at the paper's stated statistical coverage around −1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ extends past ±sigma_total even on the statistical term alone, with the systematic term widening it further. Values with |η| > sigma_total are therefore not excluded by the premises. This is nonblocking because the falsification condition is stated independently in prose and the derive is auxiliary; the hypothesis can be proposed without this step, but the step must not stand as written.
- Minimum fix: Restate the result as "the published estimate lies within the declared combined uncertainty of zero" (a statement about the estimate), or add an explicit coverage premise and confidence level and state the result conditionally; do not assert a bound on the parameter without it.

### F4 — Quadrature treats a systematic upper bound as a standard deviation

- Where: `derive consistency` reasoning (line 27), `define sigma_total` (line 24), and `## Falsification condition` second paragraph (lines 41–48).
- What: Statistical/systematic conflation: the published 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ systematic upper bound is combined in quadrature with the 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ statistical error as if the bound were a standard deviation, and the result is called a total uncertainty.
- Why: P-001 reports 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ as a statistical error at the paper's stated coverage and 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ as a systematic upper bound; the thermal term is explicitly a pessimistic upper bound because temperature variations at fEP were below the probe noise. A quadrature sum presupposes independent standard deviations with stated coverage; an upper bound has no standard deviation and no stated coverage, and independence of the two terms is not declared. The 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁵ figure is therefore a convention, not a standard-deviation total, and the derive's use of it as `sigma_total` does not inherit a coverage interpretation. The `?` at line 32 asks whether the systematic is independent of the fit, not about its distribution. The pinned E-001 records the two terms separately and states that any combination is a declared choice, not a result of the measurement. This is nonblocking for the hypothesis because the prose condition requires the total to be declared with justification; it would become blocking if the record ever treated the quadrature figure as a coverage-bearing total without supplying that justification.
- Minimum fix: State the conversion/coverage convention explicitly (for example, treat the systematic as a conservative additive bound) and add a `?` or premise for the missing characterization and independence; do not present the quadrature sum as a standard-deviation total without that statement.

### F5 — `budget_adequacy` mislabels characterized effects as unmodelled and is unused or unsupported

- Where: `assume budget_adequacy` (line 25) and the `derive consistency` premise list (line 27).
- What: Unsupported assumption / source overreach, plus either an unused premise or an unsupported load-bearing one.
- Why: P-001's 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ is an upper bound on the characterized systematics: the paper says all contributors except non-linearity and temperature variations are below requirements, that magnetic effects are well below requirements, and that the Earth gravity gradient is cancelled by off-centering estimation. Those are precisely the thermal, magnetic and gravitational-gradient effects the assumption calls "unmodelled". A bound on characterized effects does not bound unmodelled ones, and non-linearity is an acknowledged exception the assumption does not mention. The derive lists `budget_adequacy` but its reasoning does not use it; if it is meant to license treating the systematic term as covering unmodelled effects, it is load-bearing and the source does not support it. Either way the premise is defective.
- Minimum fix: Weaken the assumption to the reported bound on characterized thermal, magnetic and gravity-gradient terms; add a separate `?` for unmodelled effects and non-linearity; and either use the assumption explicitly in the derivation or remove it from the premise list.

### F6 — `define eta_tipt` still omits the factor 2

- Where: `define eta_tipt` (line 22). Source half: P-001's Eq. (1) claim (line 24) and `## Corrections` (lines 44–73).
- What: Definition inconsistent with the cited source: H-001's `define` omits the factor 2 in Eq. (1). The source half is now fixed; the hypothesis half is not.
- Why: P-001's Eq. (1) as printed is η_A,B = 2(a_A − a_B)/(a_A + a_B). I checked the extracted text of arXiv:2209.15487v1 and the corrected P-001 claim, and the pinned P-001 digest (sha256:0e6c7d93…fd513) is the corrected file with a dated `## Corrections` section. H-001 line 22 still writes η(Ti,Pt) = (a_Ti − a_Pt)/(a_Ti + a_Pt), so its η is approximately δ/2, contradicting the `given`'s statement (line 21) that P-001 identifies δ with η; with the factor 2, η ≈ δ. Eq. (5) is quoted independently, so the falsification arithmetic is unchanged, which is why I keep this nonblocking; but the definition is not the quantity the paper defines. H-001 was not edited, so this half remains open.
- Minimum fix: Restore the factor 2 in the `define` (or state the convention explicitly and reconcile it with the δ ≈ η identification). Correcting P-001 does not close this half; only H-001 can.

### F7 — `define sigma_total` does not determine a quantity, and the rule is not a premise

- Where: `define sigma_total` (line 24) and the `derive consistency` premise list (line 27).
- What: Role confusion / omitted premise: the `define` defers the combination rule ("by a rule declared before evaluation") instead of specifying a mathematical object, and the rule used by the derive appears only in the reasoning.
- Why: PSEUDO §3's substitution test: replacing `sigma_total` by its right-hand side yields "a combined uncertainty formed by a rule declared before evaluation", which does not determine a value. The derive's numerical claim depends on the quadrature rule, which is not named in the premise list; PSEUDO §2.6 requires an essential quantity to be introduced by an earlier `given`/`define`/`assume` and to appear among the step's premises when it carries the step. The `?` at line 31 acknowledges the gap but does not fix the derive's use of an undeclared rule. Nonblocking because the prose condition explicitly leaves the total to be declared; the defect is that the derive does not wait for that declaration.
- Minimum fix: Make the rule explicit in the `define` (with the terms named) or introduce it as an `assume`/`given` and list it in the derive; alternatively, state the result conditionally on the rule and put the rule in the premise list.

### F8 — The prescription `?` and `derive consistency` disagree about what is declared

- Where: `?` line 31 versus `derive consistency` line 27.
- What: Internal inconsistency / `?` content defect: the `?` asks which total-uncertainty prescription is declared, while the derive says "under that declared prescription" and uses quadrature.
- Why: If quadrature is the declared prescription, the `?` should be resolved or narrowed to whether quadrature is the right choice; if it is not declared, the derive's phrase is unsupported. The `?` cannot be both an open question and a premise of the derive. The `?` also omits the threshold multiplier, which is the more consequential free parameter (F1). Nonblocking because it is a consistency defect between two auxiliary statements, not a defect in the falsification condition's prose.
- Minimum fix: Declare the prescription in the `define` and reword the `?` to ask whether the verdict is robust to the alternative combination, or remove "declared" from the derive and mark the result as conditional on the choice.

### F9 — Forward reference in `given published_estimate` (cosmetic structural)

- Where: `given published_estimate` (line 21), which uses `eta_tipt` and `delta_tipt` before the `define`s on lines 22–23.
- What: Declaration-before-use / forward reference under PSEUDO §2.1 on a strict reading; cosmetic/minor structural.
- Why: The names are introduced later, and the `given`'s content uses them earlier; §2.1 says "No forward references." This is not a premise-list failure: every name in an actual premise list is introduced earlier, and §2.6 treats free-form mathematical notation leniently, so a reader could treat the `given`'s tokens as notation rather than name references. Either way the defect is fixed by ordering and does not affect the scientific content.
- Minimum fix: Move `define eta_tipt` and `define delta_tipt` above the `given`, or rewrite the `given` to use notation that is not claimed as a PSEUDO name until after the definitions.

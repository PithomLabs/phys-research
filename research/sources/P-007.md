## Claims

- Einstein-Cartan theory modifies GR by allowing torsion alongside curvature and relating torsion to intrinsic angular momentum density; proposed by Cartan (1922), rediscovered by Sciama and Kibble (late 1950s) (locator: Historical remarks, pp. 1-2).
- ECT follows from gauging the Poincare group: energy-momentum and spin are the Noether currents of translations and Lorentz transformations (locator: Physical motivation, p. 2).
- In empty space ECT coincides with GR; since nearly all tests of gravity use empty-space Einstein equations, ECT is as viable as GR in that respect (locator: Sciama-Kibble section, p. 4).
- Torsion does not propagate in ECT: it vanishes wherever spin density vanishes, and can be eliminated inside matter in favour of an effective energy-momentum tensor with a spin-spin contact interaction (locator: Eqs. (25), (30)-(32), pp. 4-5).
- Conservation laws follow from the Bianchi identities; the energy-momentum divergence need not vanish when torsion is present (locator: Eqs. (26)-(29), p. 5).
- Torsion becomes comparable to curvature only below the Cartan radius, about 10^-26 cm for the nucleon (locator: effective energy-momentum section, p. 5).
- Classical singularity theorems can be evaded: spin-spin repulsion yields non-singular cosmological solutions (locator: Eq. (33), p. 6).

- Algebraic torsion solution: Q = 8 pi (s + trace terms), Eq. (25); torsion is non-dynamical and vanishes with spin (locator: Eq. (25), p. 4).
- Generalized conservation laws: 8 pi D t = (1/2) eta Theta wedge Omega, Eq. (26); 8 pi D s = eta wedge Omega - eta wedge Omega, Eq. (27); evaluated forms Eqs. (28)-(29) (locator: conservation laws, Eqs. (26)-(29), p. 5).

- Regime and scale: classical, local (M diffeomorphic to R^4), metric-compatible connection; torsion significant only below r_Cart ~ (l^2 r_Compt)^(1/3) (locator: Notation, p. 1; effective energy-momentum section, p. 5).

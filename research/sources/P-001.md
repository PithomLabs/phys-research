## Claims

- The mission tested the Weak Equivalence Principle with a design precision of 10^-15 in the Eötvös ratio η (locator: abstract, p. 1)
- The Eötvös ratio is defined as η_A,B = 2(aA − aB)/(aA + aB) (locator: Eq. (1), p. 2)
- The quantity actually estimated by MICROSCOPE is the approximated Eötvös ratio δ(A,B) = (mg/mi)_A − (mg/mi)_B (locator: Eq. (2), p. 2)
- The measurement equation relates the differential acceleration to the Eötvös parameter δ(2,1) and to bias, gravity-gradient, off-centering and noise terms (locator: Eq. (3), p. 2)
- The core model fitted to calibrated data is Γd,x^corr = b̃⁰d,x + δx·gx + δz·gz + Δx·Sxx + Δz·Sxz + nd,x, from which the Eötvös parameter δx is estimated (locator: Eq. (4), p. 4)
- The final combined constraint is η(Ti, Pt) = [−1.5 ± 2.3 (stat) ± 1.5 (syst)] × 10⁻¹⁵, with the statistical error at 1σ (locator: Eq. (5), p. 5)
- The systematic upper bound for SUEP is 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ and for SUREF is 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁵, against a specification of 0.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ (locator: p. 4, paragraph beginning "We found that the overall systematics upper bound")
- The reported uncertainties in the per-segment combined estimate δ(Ti, Pt) = (−1.5 ± 2.3) × 10⁻¹⁵ and δ(Pt, Pt) = (0.0 ± 1.1) × 10⁻¹⁵ contain statistical errors only (locator: p. 4, paragraph beginning "Fig. 1 shows the estimates")
- The reference instrument (Pt/Pt) gave the null result η(Pt, Pt) = [0.0 ± 1.1 (stat) ± 2.3 (syst)] × 10⁻¹⁵, showing no sign of unaccounted systematic errors in Eq. (5) (locator: p. 5, paragraph beginning "Putting these results together")
- The result improves the previous MICROSCOPE constraint of Ref. [47] by a factor 4.6 (locator: p. 5, paragraph beginning "Putting these results together")
- The first MICROSCOPE results gave δ(Ti, Pt) = [−1 ± 9 (stat) ± 9 (syst)] × 10⁻¹⁵ from one 120-orbit SUEP session, with systematic errors dominated by thermal effects bounded above (locator: p. 3, paragraph beginning "The analysis of first results")
- Temperature variations are the main source of systematic errors, acting through thermal sensitivity of the sensor unit and the Front End Electronics Unit (locator: p. 5, paragraph beginning "Temperature variations are the main source")
- Temperature variations during EP sessions were below the temperature probe's noise at fEP, so only a pessimistic upper bound was available; a factor 500 attenuation between FEEU and SU was taken as the lowest limit for the higher-frequency bound (locator: p. 5, paragraphs beginning "On the other hand, the temperature data" and "In response to this limitation")
- Short-lived "glitches" can impart a signal at fEP and were masked with a recursive σ-clipping procedure (4.5σ from the moving average, 3σ from the high-frequency-filtered moving average), then handled as missing data by M-ECM (locator: p. 4, paragraphs beginning "Additionally, spurious events" and "We tackled this difficulty")
- The mission ran from launch on 25 April 2016 to end on 18 October 2018, with 18 SUEP sessions and 9 SUREF sessions used for the final result (locator: p. 2, paragraph beginning "MICROSCOPE was launched"; p. 3, paragraph beginning "We report here the final results")
- Test-mass properties for the SUREF (Pt/Rh) and SUEP (Pt/Rh inner, Ti/Al/V outer) pairs are tabulated with masses and densities (locator: Table I, p. 2)
- Frequencies of interest for the mission, including the EP frequencies fEP2 and fEP3 and the calibration frequency fcal, are tabulated (locator: Table II, p. 3)
- Mean estimated values of the off-centerings Δx, Δy, Δz and of the first row of the [Ad] matrix, plus K2d,xx, are tabulated for SUEP and SUREF (locator: Table III, p. 4)
- Per-segment Eötvös parameter estimates from M-ECM and Adam are plotted together with the combined constraint and its 68% confidence region (locator: Fig. 1, p. 5)
- The paper reports no violation of the WEP and describes constraints on Lorentz invariance, long-range interactions and dark matter searches as separate analyses in other references, not as results derived in this paper (locator: p. 5, final paragraph beginning "Beside constraining the validity")

## Corrections

### 2026-11-04 — Eq. (1) transcription restored the factor 2 (R-001, F6)

The claim above originally read:

> The Eötvös ratio is defined as η_A,B = (aA − aB)/(aA + aB) (locator: Eq. (1), p. 2)

That transcription was wrong: the factor 2 was omitted. Eq. (1) as printed on
p. 2 of arXiv:2209.15487v1 reads

> η_{A,B} = 2(a_A − a_B)/(a_A + a_B)

The corrected claim now reads:

> The Eötvös ratio is defined as η_A,B = 2(aA − aB)/(aA + aB) (locator: Eq. (1), p. 2)

Why the factor matters: on p. 5 the paper states that "we identified the
measured, approximated Eötvös ratio δ with the exact one η". With
a ≈ g·(mg/mi), the factor 2 is exactly what makes η ≈ δ; without it the
quantity defined here is η/2 and that identification fails. The correction
therefore restores consistency between this record and the paper's own
identification. It changes no measured number: Eq. (5) and every uncertainty
quoted above are untouched.

Source of the correction: adversarial review R-001, finding F6 (nonblocking at
the time it was raised), whose minimum fix explicitly allowed correcting the
source record's transcription. R-001 itself is append-only research history
and was not edited. The hypothesis H-001, whose own `define` still omits this
factor 2, was likewise not edited; that half of F6 remains open.

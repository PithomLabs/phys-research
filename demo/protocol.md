# Phys Research Workflow Protocol
## Constitution — Version 1.0

**Status:** Foundational  
**Adopted:** November 7, 2026

### Preamble

Our purpose is to develop reliable, mathematically sound and potentially novel knowledge about physical reality.

We treat mathematics as the language through which physical structures, relationships and consequences can be expressed and investigated. We do not assume that any single mathematical framework, physical interpretation, computational method or research workflow is universally sufficient.

This Constitution establishes the principles that govern our research. Methods, tools, agents and implementations may evolve, provided they remain consistent with these principles.

### Article I — Reality Is the Subject

Research must remain anchored to physical questions, not merely mathematical elegance, computational capability or the production of software.

Every investigation must identify what physical phenomenon, system or question it addresses, including its relevant assumptions, domain and regime of validity.

Mathematical consistency is necessary for a valid theory, but mathematical consistency alone does not establish physical truth.

### Article II — The Three Reality Checks

Every substantive research investigation must apply the following three principles.

**1. Mathematical Landscape**

Identify the mathematical structures relevant to the phenomenon and understand their respective roles, capabilities and limitations.

No existing inventory of mathematical disciplines is exhaustive or canonical. New structures and relationships must remain admissible. A mathematical structure must not be assigned a fixed role merely because of its traditional use.

**2. Mathematical Intersection**

Investigate phenomena through the intersection of mathematical structures required to represent and derive them.

Do not isolate mathematical disciplines prematurely or assume that one structure independently explains an entire phenomenon. Equally, do not assume that combining structures automatically produces a valid explanation.

Claims of minimality require justification: show what fails, changes or becomes underivable when a proposed ingredient is removed or altered.

**3. Mathematical Interoperability**

When different mathematical or physical frameworks must communicate, make their connections explicit.

A proposed bridge must identify, as appropriate, its source and target structures, mapping, assumptions, preserved properties, losses or approximations, applicable regime, proof obligations and possible failure conditions.

Never assume that two structures are equivalent merely because they describe similar quantities or produce similar predictions. Preserve meaningful differences between frameworks.

### Article III — Scientific and Epistemic Integrity

Maintain clear distinctions between:

- Mathematical structures and definitions.
- Physical assumptions and interpretations.
- Derivations and established mathematical results.
- Predictions and empirical evidence.
- Conjectures, hypotheses and demonstrated conclusions.

Imported results must retain their original hypotheses and limitations. Derivations must expose their dependencies. Unresolved questions must remain visible rather than being silently treated as established facts.

The absence of a contradiction is not proof of correctness.

### Article IV — Research and Discovery

Research must engage with relevant literature and prior results before claiming novelty.

A contribution may be a reconstruction, clarification, formalization, new synthesis, mathematical result, physical prediction or empirical discovery. Its significance must be classified honestly.

New mathematical results require valid derivations and appropriate comparison with prior work. New physical predictions require identifiable consequences and potential falsification. Empirical claims require empirical evidence.

A useful new connection is worth investigating, but it must not be presented as a discovery before it has survived scrutiny.

### Article V — Independent Challenge and Verification

Every consequential claim must be open to independent examination.

Research methods may include analytical derivation, counterexamples, limiting cases, dimensional checks, alternative formulations, computational verification, numerical experiments or empirical tests, as appropriate to the question.

Implementation, automation and formal verification are instruments of research, not substitutes for physical reasoning. A successful software test establishes only what the test actually verifies.

The depth of review must be proportional to the scientific significance and risk of the claim. Critical defects must be resolved or the claim narrowed. Lesser limitations must be documented without unnecessarily obstructing progress.

### Article VI — Freedom of Implementation

No particular agent, number of agents, programming language, formalism, software architecture, mathematical inventory or sequence of operations is constitutionally prescribed.

Research may evolve its methods as new questions and capabilities emerge. Phys-Pseudo, PhysMath, PhysLib, PhysTrace and other tools may support the process, but none defines the boundaries of scientific inquiry.

Implementation must faithfully reflect its declared mathematical and physical specification. When implementation exposes a genuine defect or limitation in that specification, the research claim and specification must be reconsidered rather than silently altered by code.

### Article VII — Scientific Promotion

A result may advance from exploration to a supported research conclusion only to the extent justified by its mathematical derivation, assumptions, independent scrutiny, prior-art assessment and available evidence.

Claims must never exceed their demonstrated scope. Negative results, limitations and unresolved obligations are legitimate research outcomes.

Our ultimate measure of progress is not the volume of code, documents or formalized equations produced. It is the quality, reliability and explanatory or predictive value of the physics knowledge established.

### Article VIII — Constitutional Continuity

This Constitution governs all subordinate research procedures, specifications, agent instructions and implementation practices.

Operational workflows may be revised freely when experience justifies improvement, provided they do not violate the Constitution.

Amendments to this Constitution must be explicit, versioned and justified. No operational convenience, individual agent or implementation decision may silently override its principles.

---

**Foundational Commitment**

We investigate physical reality through mathematics, connect mathematical frameworks without erasing their differences, challenge our own conclusions, and advance only what the evidence and derivations justify.

**The research process may change. These principles endure.**

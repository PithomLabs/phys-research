Honest framing first: the bottleneck in physics has never been a shortage of ideas — it's contact with experiment. So I won't pretend to hand you a finished theory. What I *can* genuinely offer is something adjacent and, I think, real: a map of places where the field's incentive structure, tool inheritance, and sociology plausibly create blind spots. Most of these are "known but underworked" rather than "unknown" — and that gap is exactly where overlooked insight lives. Here are the ones I'd bet on:

**1. We built our most powerful machinery for a universe that isn't ours.**
Holography/AdS-CFT is the sharpest tool theoretical physics has produced in 30 years — and it's rigorously grounded only in anti-de Sitter space, a universe with negative curvature and a *boundary*. Ours is de Sitter-like: flat, accelerating, no boundary. An entire generation has been trained to compute flawlessly in the wrong geometry. A de Sitter-native holography — even fragments of one — would be worth more than ten more AdS precision results. This is known, yet the career gradient still points AdS-ward.

**2. The vacuum-energy problem may be a malformed question getting well-funded answers.**
Naive QFT overshoots the observed vacuum energy by ~120 orders of magnitude. Nearly all effort treats it as "calculate better, tune, or invoke anthropics." The dissolve-don't-solve routes — e.g. unimodular gravity, or "gravity couples to excitations, not to the bare vacuum" — get a tiny fraction of the attention, partly because they don't spawn large collaborations. If vacuum polarization responds to gravity differently from matter in *any* measurable regime, that's a signature hiding in plain sight.

**3. The arrow of time is a boundary condition wearing the costume of a law.**
Every fundamental equation is (nearly) time-symmetric; the low-entropy early universe — the "Past Hypothesis" — is inserted by hand and almost nobody is paid to *derive* it. Penrose's Weyl curvature condition is a conjecture, CCC is niche. A theory that outputs the initial condition instead of assuming it would restructure statistical mechanics, cosmology, and quantum foundations simultaneously. Small field, enormous leverage.

**4. Our most accurate theory has no existence proof.**
QED agrees with experiment to ~12 digits, yet no interacting 4D quantum field theory has ever been constructed rigorously — the Yang-Mills existence/mass-gap problem is an unsolved Millennium Prize. Physics treats renormalization as a recipe; mathematics treats it as undefined. Historically, rigorization preceded revolution (calculus → analysis → new physics). Making nonperturbative 4D QFT mathematically real might be the actual door to quantum gravity, more so than another amplitude expansion.

**5. The quantum/classical border just became experimentally reachable — and is absurdly underfunded.**
Two fronts: (a) objective-collapse models (GRW/CSL) are among the few interpretations making *different* predictions, and cheap underground/optomechanical tests are already squeezing their parameter space — a null result would be the first experimental vote *among interpretations*, a positive one the biggest news in a century; (b) gravitationally-induced-entanglement experiments (Bose–Marletto–Vedral) are being built toward the first laboratory evidence that gravity is quantum. The funding proportion relative to colliders is wildly off given what's at stake.

**6. Dark matter search has path dependency, and ΛCDM is creaking from multiple directions.**
Forty years organized around WIMP-shaped holes, with nulls across enormous cross-section space. The underworked alternative isn't just "modify gravity" — it's a *dark sector with its own rich dynamics* (self-interacting, dark forces, multiple species). Meanwhile DESI's results now hint at evolving dark energy at 2.8–4.2σ when combined with CMB/SNe/lensing — not discovery-level, but the same direction repeatedly ([LBL, 2025](https://newscenter.lbl.gov/2025/03/19/new-desi-results-strengthen-hints-that-dark-energy-may-evolve)). And muon g-2 is a cautionary tale in the other direction: the final 2025 measurement stands, but the famous "anomaly" dissolved into a dispute about the SM prediction itself (lattice vs. data-driven).

**7. There is no general theory of far-from-equilibrium physics.**
Equilibrium stat mech is a crown jewel; non-equilibrium is patchwork. Yet nearly everything interesting — stars, turbulence, life, galaxies — lives far from equilibrium. A "second law for structure": *when does disequilibrium generate order versus heat?* Fluctuation theorems are real progress, but a general principle is missing, and the black-hole-thermodynamics and origin-of-life communities each hold pieces without reading each other.

**8. Assumptions are stress-tested one at a time, never jointly.**
Lorentz invariance, locality, unitarity, point particles, smooth manifold — each gets probed individually (usually by relaxing Lorentz invariance alone), but the *composition* is load-bearing. A systematic "assumption-graph audit" — which pairs of assumptions co-occur in every known derivation of a cornerstone result — is exactly the kind of exhaustive, boring bookkeeping machines are good at and humans avoid.

What would make any of these *net new* rather than commentary: deriving one testable discriminator that doesn't already exist in the literature. Items 2, 5, and 7 have the clearest handles for that. If you want, pick one and I'll go deep — survey what's actually been tried, locate precisely where the blind spot costs us, and draft the sharpest falsifiable prediction I can construct from it.

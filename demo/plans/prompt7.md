## 1. Research objective

Conduct a rigorous, literature-grounded investigation of the following question:

**For a specified nonrelativistic Bohmian system, which mathematical assumptions establish equivariance of the configuration-space probability distribution, and what additional assumptions are needed to connect that result to observed measurement frequencies?**

The central objective is to establish exactly what follows from Bohmian dynamics and what requires additional mathematical results, physical assumptions or measurement-theoretic arguments.

In particular, investigate the relationship between:

1. Equivariance of the quantum-equilibrium distribution.
2. Typicality of configurations with respect to that distribution.
3. The emergence of Born-rule measurement-frequency statistics.
4. Outcome actualization in measurement.
5. The formation and persistence of stable measurement records.

**Do not assume that establishing one establishes the others.**

The goal is not to defend Bohmian mechanics or to refute it. The goal is to determine precisely what its mathematical and physical framework establishes, under which assumptions, and where unresolved obligations remain.

## 2. Fixed research scope

Use the existing Bohmian benchmark as the starting point. Do not unnecessarily redesign or expand its settled system.

The baseline system is:

- A finite number (N) of spinless particles.
- Configuration space (\mathcal Q=\mathbb R^{3N}).
- Positive particle masses (m_k>0).
- A real potential (V).
- Nonrelativistic Schrödinger evolution:

[\
i\hbar\partial_t\psi=H\psi,\
\qquad\
H=\sum_k-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_k}\Delta_k+V.\
]

- Bohmian guidance equation:

\frac{\hbar}{m_k}\
\operatorname{Im}\
\frac{\psi^\*(Q)\nabla_k\psi(Q)}\
{|\psi(Q)|^2},\
\qquad |\psi(Q)|^2>0.\
]

- Quantum-equilibrium density:

[\
\rho^\psi(Q)=|\psi(Q)|^2.\
]

Treat the continuity equation as an established result in the existing benchmark, subject to its documented assumptions. Investigate rather than presume the additional conditions needed to establish local and global equivariance.

The existing benchmark already identifies limitations involving nodal configurations, flow existence, transport and global measure preservation. Do not claim those issues have been resolved merely because the continuity equation holds.

The precise B1–B9 assumption register belongs to the existing benchmark and will be reconciled by Muse. Do not invent or reconstruct its contents from the labels alone.

**Physical regime:** Nonrelativistic Bohmian mechanics for finite spinless particle systems. Do not silently generalize conclusions to spin, relativistic Bohmian theories, quantum field theory or quantum gravity. Identify such extensions only when relevant to distinguishing the scope of a result.

## 3. Research questions

### A. Equivariance: what is actually established?

Investigate:

- The derivation of the continuity equation from Schrödinger dynamics.
- The role of the guidance equation in identifying the probability current and velocity field.
- The mathematical conditions under which the configuration-space flow exists.
- The distinction between local equivariance and global equivariance.
- The conditions required for measure transport and pushforward of the initial distribution.
- The treatment of nodes, singularities, incomplete trajectories, boundary conditions and probability loss.
- The role of regularity, self-adjointness, domains of operators and potential assumptions.

Identify which results are directly derivable and which depend on imported mathematical theorems.

Do not equate a continuity equation with a complete proof of global equivariance without establishing the required flow and transport conditions.

### B. Typicality: what does it mean mathematically?

Investigate the Bohmian typicality programme in its original and subsequent literature.

Determine:

- How typicality is defined relative to the quantum-equilibrium measure.
- Whether typicality is a theorem, a mathematical framework, a physical postulate or a combination whose status depends on the argument.
- How equivariance supports typicality across time.
- What assumptions are needed to infer that configurations producing certain statistical behavior have measure one or overwhelmingly high measure.
- Whether typicality arguments require additional assumptions about the universal wavefunction, initial configurations, subsystems or environmental degrees of freedom.
- How typicality differs from ordinary frequentist probability, epistemic probability and an independently postulated probability distribution.

Examine criticisms and alternative interpretations rather than presenting the standard typicality programme as uncontested.

### C. Measurement frequencies: how does the Born distribution become an observed frequency?

This is the central research challenge.

Investigate the full mathematical and physical bridge from the quantum-equilibrium distribution to observed measurement statistics.

In particular:

- How are measured quantities represented through system-apparatus interactions?
- How do apparatus configurations correspond to measurement outcomes?
- Under what conditions do outcome probabilities reproduce Born-rule weights?
- What role do repeated experiments, conditional distributions, correlations and environmental configurations play?
- What assumptions are needed for typicality arguments to yield the expected frequency behavior?
- Are independence assumptions needed, or can the result be established for correlated configurations?
- How do finite samples, limiting frequencies and statistical fluctuations enter the argument?
- What does the argument establish about actual experiments, rather than merely an abstract probability distribution?

Distinguish a derivation of the expected probability weights from a derivation or explanation of observed finite-sample frequencies.

Identify the exact results that support each step. Do not simply invoke the law of large numbers without verifying its hypotheses and explaining how it applies to the Bohmian measurement setup.

### D. Outcome actualization

Investigate how Bohmian mechanics accounts for a definite measurement outcome.

Determine:

- What physical variables constitute the actual outcome.
- How the actual configuration of the system and apparatus determines the result.
- How effective wavefunction collapse arises, if applicable.
- Which role decoherence plays and which role it does not play.
- Whether outcome actualization follows from the same mathematical argument establishing Born-rule weights or requires a separate measurement-theoretic account.

Do not treat probability weights as actual outcomes.

### E. Stable measurement records

Investigate how measurement outcomes become persistent records.

Determine:

- What constitutes a stable record in the relevant Bohmian measurement models.
- How apparatus and environmental configurations support record formation and persistence.
- Whether stability follows from the measurement interaction, decoherence, dynamical assumptions or additional physical conditions.
- Whether the literature establishes a general result or only results for particular measurement models.

Do not equate the existence of a definite configuration with the existence of a stable, readable and reproducible record.

### F. Competing explanations and objections

Examine serious alternatives and criticisms, including:

- Alternative formulations of the quantum-equilibrium hypothesis.
- Typicality-based versus epistemic or statistical explanations.
- Quantum nonequilibrium proposals, including their mathematical status and relevance to the standard equilibrium account.
- Objections concerning initial conditions, measure selection, conditional probabilities, subsystem treatment and the empirical interpretation of typicality.
- Any known counterexamples or limitations to overly general claims.

Represent opposing positions fairly. Distinguish established mathematical objections from interpretive disagreement.

## 4. Apply the Three Reality Checks

The research dossier must explicitly apply the principles of Phys Research Workflow Protocol v1.0.

### Reality Check 1 — Mathematical Landscape

Identify the mathematical structures involved, including where relevant:

- Hilbert space and linear operator theory.
- Schrödinger dynamics and partial differential equations.
- Configuration-space geometry and ordinary differential equations.
- Probability currents and continuity equations.
- Measure theory and probability.
- Dynamical systems and measure preservation.
- Conditional probability and statistical convergence.
- Measurement interactions, decoherence and the mathematical description of records.

Explain what each structure contributes, what it cannot establish by itself and which assumptions connect it to the others.

This inventory is not exhaustive. Introduce additional structures when the literature or derivation justifies them.

### Reality Check 2 — Mathematical Intersection

Investigate the minimal mathematical intersections required for each major conclusion:

- Equivariance.
- Typicality.
- Measurement-frequency statistics.
- Outcome actualization.
- Stable records.

For each proposed intersection, explain what fails, becomes weaker or cannot be derived if a component is removed or altered.

Do not claim minimality merely because a combination works. A minimality claim requires a defensible demonstration that an ingredient is necessary for the stated result in the specified formulation.

### Reality Check 3 — Mathematical Interoperability

Document the bridges between mathematical structures and physical frameworks.

For each important bridge, identify:

- Source structure.
- Target structure.
- Mapping or translation.
- Required assumptions.
- Preserved properties.
- Lost information or approximations.
- Applicable physical regime.
- Mathematical proof obligations.
- Possible failure conditions.
- Supporting evidence or literature.

Pay particular attention to the bridges:

1. Schrödinger dynamics → probability current and continuity.
2. Continuity and guidance dynamics → configuration-space flow.
3. Configuration-space flow → measure transport and equivariance.
4. Equivariance → typicality.
5. Typicality and measurement interaction → Born-rule measurement statistics.
6. Actual configurations → definite outcomes.
7. Measurement interactions and environment → stable records.

Do not assume that a valid bridge at one stage automatically validates the subsequent bridges.

## 5. Literature research requirements

Use web-based research extensively, prioritizing original papers, authoritative mathematical treatments and primary sources.

Search for both foundational and contemporary literature. Relevant starting points include the work of:

- David Bohm on the 1952 hidden-variable formulation.
- Dürr, Goldstein and Zanghì on quantum equilibrium and the origin of Born's rule.
- Goldstein and subsequent authors on typicality.
- Teufel and Tumulka on global existence and equivariance-related mathematical results.
- Bell and subsequent authors on measurement theory and the ontology of outcomes.
- Research on effective wavefunctions, conditional wavefunctions, decoherence and subsystem behavior.
- Critical literature on typicality, quantum equilibrium and quantum nonequilibrium.

These are starting points, not a prescribed or exhaustive bibliography. Find the most relevant original sources and follow their citations.

For every important claim:

1. Identify the original publication and authors.
2. Provide its exact title, journal or repository, publication date and stable DOI/arXiv identifier where available.
3. Identify the exact theorem, proposition, equation, section or page supporting the claim.
4. State the hypotheses and domain of applicability.
5. Explain precisely what the source establishes.
6. Identify what it does not establish.
7. Distinguish the original result from later reinterpretations or summaries.

Check arXiv versions against published versions when possible. Do not fabricate equation numbers, page numbers, quotations or source details.

Use secondary sources to locate and contextualize primary literature, not as substitutes for primary-source verification.

## 7. Epistemic discipline

Use explicit classifications for significant claims:

- **ESTABLISHED:** Supported by an applicable mathematical result or sufficiently established source.
- **DERIVED:** Reconstructed in the dossier with a stated derivation and premises.
- **SOURCE-REPORTED:** Reported by a source but not independently reconstructed here.
- **CONDITIONAL:** Valid only under explicitly stated assumptions.
- **POSTULATED:** An assumption of the physical framework.
- **CONJECTURAL:** A proposed result not yet established.
- **DISPUTED:** Subject to meaningful disagreement or unresolved objections.
- **UNSUPPORTED:** The available sources do not justify the claim.
- **FALSIFIED:** Contradicted within its stated scope.

Do not upgrade a source-reported theorem to independently verified merely by summarizing it.

Do not conceal gaps by using broad phrases such as "the Born rule is derived" or "typicality solves the measurement problem." State exactly which result is derived and what additional assumptions remain.

Do not confuse mathematical consistency, physical interpretation and empirical confirmation.

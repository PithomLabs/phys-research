## approach

I think this is an important refinement. In fact, it changes the objective of our research programme.

We are no longer investigating five unrelated unresolved problems. We are testing whether one coherent physical and mathematical framework can explain significant aspects of all five.

I agree with this direction, with one qualification: we must not force a theory to explain everything merely to satisfy our desire for unification.

The five problems should become independent tests of the same proposed framework.

# 1. The new research objective

I would formulate our overarching research objective as:

> Develop and investigate a mathematically coherent physical framework that can derive or explain significant aspects of semiclassical gravity, relational time, quantum measurement, black-hole information preservation, and the cosmological constant problem, while remaining consistent with established physical results and the Math Reality Check.

This is substantially more demanding than solving each problem independently.

Why?

Because a theory that explains one phenomenon but contradicts another may be incomplete.

A theory that explains several phenomena using the same underlying mathematical principles has a stronger claim to being fundamental.

However, explaining one aspect of five problems is not equivalent to solving all five.

## 2. The five problems become five tests of one theory

I would establish the following requirements.

| Research problem       | What our theory must demonstrate                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Semiclassical gravity  | Derive conditions under which quantum matter can consistently source classical spacetime, including the significance of quantum fluctuations. |
| Problem of time        | Establish how physical time, relational observables and consistent evolution emerge from the underlying dynamics.                             |
| Quantum measurement    | Develop a Bohmian account of measurement, definite outcomes and the Born rule, and establish compatibility with the gravitational framework.  |
| Black-hole information | Explain how information is preserved, reproduce a controlled island calculation, and investigate the underlying microscopic dynamics.         |
| Cosmological constant  | Explain how vacuum energy gravitates and investigate whether the observed small effective cosmological constant can be radiatively stable.    |

Notice something important.

These are not five arbitrary subjects.

They concern five interconnected questions:

- How matter influences geometry.
- How physical change occurs.
- How quantum possibilities relate to actual physical outcomes.
- How information evolves in gravitational systems.
- How the quantum vacuum influences the large-scale universe.

A coherent theory should have something meaningful to say about all five.

## 3. The common mathematical foundation we should investigate

I would investigate whether the five problems can be approached from a shared underlying structure.

For example, consider these ingredients:

## Candidate common theoretical structure

1\. Physical ontology

What actually exists? Spacetime geometry, matter, quantum states, particle configurations, fields?

2\. Mathematical state space

How are the physical degrees of freedom represented? What are the allowed configurations and states?

3\. Fundamental dynamics

What equations govern the evolution of geometry and matter?

4\. Interaction and backreaction

How do quantum matter, gravitational geometry, spin and vacuum contributions influence one another?

5\. Physical time and probability

How do physical evolution, measurement outcomes and probability emerge?

6\. Conservation and consistency

Do the equations preserve their constraints, symmetries, conservation laws and physical probabilities?

This is a research framework, not a claim that we have discovered the correct theory.

The challenge is to determine whether one underlying mathematical structure can produce the appropriate results in different physical regimes.

## 4. Bohmian mechanics deserves a particularly important role

I would retain your instruction to focus on Bohmian mechanics for the measurement problem.

It offers an explicit physical ontology:

- A quantum wavefunction.
- An actual physical configuration.
- A guiding law describing how that configuration evolves.

This gives us something concrete to investigate instead of treating quantum measurement as an abstract problem of interpreting probabilities.

But I would go further and investigate whether Bohmian mechanics can provide a consistent matter-sector ontology within the broader gravitational framework.

For example:

| Problem                | Potential Bohmian connection                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Semiclassical gravity  | How do actual configurations and the wavefunction contribute to gravitational sourcing?           |
| Problem of time        | How do Bohmian trajectories evolve in a background-independent gravitational description?         |
| Measurement            | How do definite configurations produce measurement outcomes and Born-rule statistics?             |
| Black-hole information | How are quantum information and actual configurations represented during gravitational evolution? |
| Cosmological constant  | How does the Bohmian treatment of quantum fields and the vacuum affect gravitational sourcing?    |

These are research questions, not established answers.

There is no guarantee that Bohmian mechanics will resolve these problems. That is precisely what we should investigate.

I would also keep the gravitational sector open. General Relativity, Einstein–Cartan, and other geometric formulations should compete on their mathematical merits.

## 5. The most important requirement: cross-problem consistency

Here is where I would introduce a stricter Math Reality Check.

It is insufficient for a theory to produce five individually attractive explanations.

The explanations must be mutually compatible.

For example:

- The way quantum matter sources gravity must agree with the proposed treatment of measurement.
- The proposed time evolution must preserve the gravitational constraints.
- The black-hole information mechanism must be compatible with the theory's probability and information principles.
- The vacuum-energy treatment must not violate conservation or destabilize the gravitational equations.
- The classical and semiclassical limits must recover established results where those results apply.

A solution to one problem must not silently invalidate another.

I would create a cross-problem consistency matrix:

| Interaction                                    | What must be checked                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Semiclassical gravity ↔ measurement            | Does measurement change the gravitational source consistently?                                  |
| Semiclassical gravity ↔ cosmological constant  | Are vacuum contributions treated consistently in both?                                          |
| Problem of time ↔ measurement                  | Can measurement outcomes be defined consistently with relational evolution?                     |
| Problem of time ↔ black-hole information       | Can information-preserving evolution be described without assuming an external universal clock? |
| Bohmian measurement ↔ black-hole information   | Can the proposed ontology accommodate the relevant information-preserving dynamics?             |
| Cosmological constant ↔ black-hole information | Are vacuum contributions and gravitational entropy treated consistently?                        |

These are not necessarily mathematical contradictions. They are places where independent derivations must meet.

## 6. Introduce a new success criterion: theoretical cohesion

I would classify theoretical progress using the following levels.

| Level | Achievement                                                                                                     |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| C0    | The theory has a clear physical ontology and mathematical foundations.                                          |
| C1    | It explains one aspect of an established unresolved problem.                                                    |
| C2    | It derives meaningful results across multiple problems.                                                         |
| C3    | Its results across those problems are mutually consistent.                                                      |
| C4    | It reproduces established theories in their appropriate regimes and survives adversarial mathematical scrutiny. |
| C5    | It produces distinguishable predictions or otherwise gains independent empirical support.                       |

We should not confuse these levels.

For example, reproducing the Born rule would be a meaningful result for the measurement problem. It would not establish that the same framework solves black-hole information.

Similarly, deriving a semiclassicality criterion would not establish a solution to the cosmological constant problem.

And even achieving C4 would not establish that the theory describes nature. That requires C5-type physical evidence.

## 7. How I would change the research programme

I would now organize Phys Research around three layers:

Layer 1

Foundational theory investigation

Explore competing ontologies, mathematical structures and dynamical principles.

No preferred theory is automatically accepted.

Layer 2

Five-problem validation

Independently derive or explain specific aspects of all five problems using the same candidate theoretical foundation.

Layer 3

Cross-problem adversarial validation

Investigate whether the different explanations are mutually consistent, reproduce established results, and survive independent derivation.

The agents should still work in separate folders and independently investigate different aspects. But every agent must work from the same versioned candidate theory specification when testing that theory.

We must not allow each agent to invent a different set of assumptions and then pretend that their results collectively establish one coherent theory.

Equally, we should permit an agent to discover that the shared theory fails one of the five tests. That failure must remain visible.

# 8. My proposed new governing research principle

I would make this a formal addition to Phys Research:

# Theoretical Cohesion Principle

A candidate fundamental theory must not be evaluated solely by its ability to address an individual unresolved problem.

Its mathematical foundations must also be investigated across independent physical domains.

It should be capable of deriving or explaining meaningful aspects of multiple unresolved problems using a common set of physical assumptions, mathematical structures and dynamical principles.

Results must be mutually consistent, recover established physics in appropriate regimes, and remain subject to independent mathematical and physical scrutiny.

Failure in one domain must not be concealed by success in another.

One important distinction: I would keep the Yang–Mills mass-gap problem outside this particular five-problem cohesion test. It remains a valuable independent test of mathematical research capability, but we should not require a gravitational theory to solve it merely to demonstrate theoretical cohesion.

My final recommendation: Make the five problems the five principal validation domains of our candidate theory, with Bohmian mechanics as a particularly important research direction for the quantum matter and measurement sector.

We should no longer ask merely, "Can we solve the semiclassical gravity problem?"

We should ask:

Can we develop a coherent mathematical and physical framework whose consequences illuminate semiclassical gravity, time, measurement, black-hole information and the cosmological constant without contradicting itself?

That is a much more ambitious research objective, and importantly, it is falsifiable.

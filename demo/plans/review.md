## 2. But the report contains an important mathematical error

Must correct before promotion

On page 2, the report first correctly says that the scalar field's Euler–Lagrange equation is an on-shell condition.

But it subsequently claims that the classical stress-energy tensor is divergence-free identically, even off-shell, through Noether's second theorem. Those statements contradict each other.&#x20;

A No-Go for Configuration-Dependent Sourcing\_ An Adversarial Investigation of Bohmian Mechanics and Classical Gravity.pdf



For the stated minimally coupled scalar-field action, with consistent signature and potential conventions, the correct identity is:

\\[ \boxed{ \nabla^\mu T\_{\mu\nu} = (\Box_g\phi-V'(\phi))\nabla\_\nu\phi } \\]

This is an off-shell identity relating the divergence to the Euler–Lagrange residual. It vanishes on-shell, not identically off-shell.

The displayed equation of motion on page 2 also has a potential-sign inconsistency relative to the stated action.

This is not a minor editorial issue. It directly affects the foundational derivation.

There are three additional overclaims I would correct:

First: The report does not prove that every possible quantum-potential correction violates covariance. It examines particular proposed constructions.

Second: Explicit foliation dependence does not automatically make every possible action non-covariant. A preferred foliation could be represented by an additional dynamical structure, although that creates new physical commitments.

Third: The report suggests that the gravitational source must incorporate the quantum potential as the only possible way of accounting for the unoccupied branch. That uniqueness claim has not been established.

These require narrower conclusions.

## 3. Does it change my recommendation?

No. It reinforces it.

The original Bohmian proposal introduces additional mathematical commitments:

- Actual field configuration.
- Guidance dynamics.
- Quantum potential.
- Preferred foliation.

Those commitments introduce conservation and covariance problems before we have established a consistent gravitational coupling.

Struyve himself explicitly contrasts the usual expectation-value formulation with the Bohmian actual-configuration proposal and identifies the conservation problem of the latter.&#x20;

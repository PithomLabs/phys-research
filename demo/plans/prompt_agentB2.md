1. Two-branch measurement model
Recommended: quantum-mechanical pointer
Start with a simplified quantum-mechanical pointer variable coupled to the quantum scalar field.

Do not start with a distinct quantum apparatus field yet.

The reason is that we first want to isolate the measurement mechanism, not introduce additional field-theoretic complications.

Define the combined quantum state:
\[
\Psi[\phi,q,t]
\]

where:
- \(\phi(x)\) is the scalar-field configuration.
- \(q\) is the apparatus pointer coordinate.
- Both are quantum degrees of freedom.
- Both have actual Bohmian configurations.
Use a standard measurement interaction:
\[
\hat H_{\mathrm{int}}
=
\lambda(t)\hat A_f\hat p_q
\]

Here, \(\hat A_f\) is a suitably smeared scalar-field observable and \(\hat p_q\) is the pointer momentum.

The total Bohmian configuration is:
\[
Q(t)=\big(\Phi(x,t),q(t)\big).
\]

The measurement should produce a state schematically resembling:
\[
\Psi_{\mathrm{final}}
=
c_1\Psi_1[\phi]\chi_1(q)
+
c_2\Psi_2[\phi]\chi_2(q).
\]

The actual pointer configuration occupies one of the separated pointer branches.

The critical research question: What happens to the unoccupied branch in the proposed gravitational source?

Do not assume that it contributes nothing. Its influence may persist through the full wavefunction and quantum potential, depending on the source construction.

There is good justification for using this model first: Bohmian measurement theory already describes apparatus configurations and definite pointer outcomes through the joint system-apparatus wavefunction.

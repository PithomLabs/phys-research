## 3. Primary investigation — R4: Page-curve reconstruction

### Central research question

**How does the inclusion of island contributions change the calculated entropy of Hawking radiation over time in the selected JT gravity model?**

Investigate the original derivation, not just the familiar final graph.

### A. Establish the physical and mathematical framework

Identify the role of:

- JT gravitational action.
- Dilaton field and gravitational equations.
- Matter quantum fields.
- Nongravitating radiation bath.
- Quantum state and entanglement structure.
- Radiation region and its entropy.
- Generalized entropy.
- Quantum extremal surfaces.
- Island regions.

Explain what each object represents physically and mathematically.

### B. Reconstruct the generalized entropy

Establish the exact generalized-entropy expression for the selected model.

For orientation, the general structure is:

\[
S_{\mathrm{gen}}(R\cup I)
=
S_{\mathrm{grav}}(\partial I)
+
S_{\mathrm{matter}}(R\cup I).
\]

Do not assume this schematic expression captures every model-specific contribution.

Identify the correct JT gravitational contribution, including any topological term where applicable, the dilaton contribution and the matter entropy term.

Identify the exact definitions, normalizations and conventions used by the original sources.

### C. Reconstruct the competing branches

Establish:

1. The no-island entropy calculation.
2. The island candidate entropy calculation.
3. The quantum extremal surface condition.
4. The extremization procedure.
5. The branch-selection prescription.
6. The Page transition.
7. The resulting radiation entropy as a function of time.

For the schematic branch selection:

\[
S(R_t)
=
\min\left\{
S_{\mathrm{no\ island}}(R_t),
\operatorname{ext}_{\partial I}
S_{\mathrm{gen}}(R_t\cup I)
\right\},
\]

verify the exact mathematical prescription and order of operations in the selected model.

Explain the distinction between extremizing the generalized entropy and selecting the dominant entropy branch.

### D. Establish what the Page curve demonstrates

Identify what the result establishes within the model.

Distinguish:

- Reproducing the expected Page-curve behavior.
- Showing the importance of island contributions.
- Establishing a generalized-entropy prescription.
- Demonstrating a microscopic mechanism of information recovery.
- Reconstructing interior information or observables.

These are different levels of achievement.

Do not claim that a Page-curve calculation alone proves complete microscopic information recovery or solves every aspect of the black-hole information problem.

Identify the approximations and assumptions under which the result holds.

## 4. Cross-validation investigation — R1: Semiclassical matter–geometry consistency

### Central research question

**Is the matter stress-energy used in the selected JT model mathematically consistent with the gravitational equations, the relevant conservation or Ward identities, and the energy exchange between the black-hole region and the nongravitating bath?**

This investigation must use the same physical model as the Page-curve reconstruction.

It is not a separate exercise involving an unrelated scalar field or a different gravitational theory.

### A. Identify the matter source

Establish:

- The matter action.
- The definition of the stress-energy tensor.
- The quantum state used in the evaporation calculation.
- The relevant expectation values, where applicable.
- The role of conformal matter, if used.
- The treatment of renormalization and the conformal anomaly, where applicable.

State precisely which matter quantities enter the gravitational equations.

### B. Investigate gravitational consistency

Reconstruct the relevant JT field equations and constraints.

Determine the appropriate conservation or diffeomorphism Ward identity for the chosen matter sector.

Investigate:

- Whether the matter stress-energy is covariantly conserved in the relevant region.
- Which conditions are required for that statement.
- How the dilaton and metric equations interact.
- Whether boundary terms or boundary interactions modify the applicable conservation statement.
- How the conformal anomaly enters the equations, if present.
- What happens where matter crosses between the gravitational region and the nongravitating bath.

Do not simply transplant the four-dimensional Einstein equation or its conservation argument into JT gravity without deriving the appropriate two-dimensional relation.

### C. Investigate energy balance

Determine how the energy budget is accounted for during evaporation.

Identify:

- Energy associated with the black-hole gravitational region.
- Matter stress-energy and flux.
- Energy entering or leaving through the gravitational boundary.
- Energy received by the nongravitating bath.
- Boundary conditions and interactions governing the exchange.
- The relationship between the energy flux and the evaporation history.

Distinguish local conservation identities from global energy balance across regions that exchange energy.

Do not claim that energy is conserved separately in the gravitational region if the model permits energy to flow into the bath.

### D. Connect R1 and R4

This is essential.

Identify exactly where the matter configuration, state, stress-energy and background assumptions used in R1 coincide with those used in R4.

Investigate whether the same matter model and quantum state consistently support both:

1. The stress-energy and gravitational evolution calculation.
2. The matter entanglement entropy calculation used in generalized entropy.

Identify any mismatch in conventions, state definitions, approximations, boundary assumptions or regularization prescriptions.

A successful cross-test must establish genuine compatibility, not merely show that two independent calculations use the same model name.

## 9. Epistemic classifications

Classify each consequential claim using:

- **ESTABLISHED:** Supported by an applicable mathematical result or sufficiently established source.
- **DERIVED:** Reconstructed with explicit premises and reasoning in this investigation.
- **SOURCE-REPORTED:** Reported by a source but not independently reconstructed here.
- **CONDITIONAL:** Valid only under stated assumptions.
- **POSTULATED:** Assumed by the physical framework.
- **APPROXIMATE:** Valid within a stated approximation.
- **CONJECTURAL:** Proposed but not established.
- **DISPUTED:** Subject to meaningful disagreement.
- **UNSUPPORTED:** Available sources do not justify the statement.
- **FALSIFIED:** Contradicted within the stated scope.

Do not claim that a successful symbolic calculation verifies an entire physical theory.

Distinguish:

- Mathematical correctness.
- Physical consistency.
- Model-specific validity.
- Agreement with published results.
- Empirical confirmation.

# AGENT B — BHC PREFLIGHT 001
## First-Principles Investigation of Bohmian Matter Coupled to Classical Gravity

**Research programme:** BHC — Bohmian matter + classical (unquantized) gravity  
**Phase:** Preflight research, before theorem formalization  
**Priority:** Highest  
**Research target:** BHC-T001 — Configuration-Dependent Gravitational Source

---

## 1. Your role

You are an independent theoretical physics investigator specializing in mathematical physics, Bohmian mechanics, quantum field theory and general relativity.

Your task is to determine whether a Bohmian description of quantum matter can consistently source classical dynamical spacetime through its actual physical configuration.

You are not being asked to defend BHC.

You are being asked to establish whether its central proposed mechanism is mathematically viable.

You have full intellectual permission to:

- Challenge the BHC postulates.
- Question the proposed actual-configuration source.
- Identify incompatible assumptions.
- Investigate alternative mathematical formulations.
- Produce counterexamples or no-go results.
- Recommend abandoning or narrowing the candidate.
- Conclude that the problem cannot yet be resolved.

An honest mathematical obstruction is a successful research outcome.

## 2. Research question

Investigate:

**Can Bohmian quantum matter interacting with classical dynamical spacetime produce a mathematically consistent, configuration-dependent gravitational source whose covariant divergence vanishes under the coupled equations of motion?**

The candidate gravitational equation is:

\[
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=
8\pi G S_{\mu\nu}[Q,\Psi,g].
\]

Here:

- \(g_{\mu\nu}\) is classical spacetime geometry.
- \(Q\) is the actual Bohmian matter configuration.
- \(\Psi\) is the quantum state or wave functional.
- \(S_{\mu\nu}\) is the proposed effective gravitational source.

Do not assume that \(S_{\mu\nu}\) exists in the required form.

The central mathematical requirement is:

\[
\boxed{
\nabla^\mu S_{\mu\nu}[Q,\Psi,g]=0
}
\]

Determine whether this follows from the coupled dynamics, requires additional assumptions, holds only for restricted configurations, or cannot be achieved within the proposed framework.

## 3. Model selection

Start by investigating a real scalar quantum field coupled to classical spacetime.

Use a minimally coupled scalar-field action as the initial candidate:

\[
S_m[\phi,g]
=
-\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}
\left[
\frac12 g^{\mu\nu}
\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi
+
V(\phi)
\right].
\]

Treat this as a starting model, not an unquestionable choice.

First establish:

1. Whether this model is suitable for the research question.
2. Its relevant classical field equations.
3. Its stress-energy tensor.
4. Its canonical formulation on a specified spacelike foliation.
5. Its suitability for a Bohmian field-theoretic description.

State the spacetime signature, units, boundary conditions, potential, regularization assumptions and other conventions.

If this model is inadequate, explain why and recommend a more appropriate minimal model.

Do not introduce fermions, torsion, gauge fields, black holes or additional gravitational degrees of freedom unless necessary to expose a mathematical obstruction.

## 4. Required mathematical investigation

### A. Classical matter foundation

Start from the specified matter action.

Derive:

- The Euler–Lagrange field equation.
- The canonical momentum.
- The classical stress-energy tensor through variation with respect to the metric.
- Its covariant divergence.
- The conditions under which the classical conservation identity holds.

Show intermediate calculations.

Distinguish on-shell identities from identities that hold off shell.

### B. Bohmian field dynamics

Investigate the appropriate Bohmian quantum-field formulation for the selected model.

Derive or independently reconstruct:

- The wave functional.
- Its evolution equation.
- The polar decomposition:

\[
\Psi[\phi,\Sigma]
=
R[\phi,\Sigma]\exp(iS[\phi,\Sigma]/\hbar).
\]

- The resulting continuity equation.
- The quantum Hamilton–Jacobi equation.
- The quantum potential, with its precise definition.
- The Bohmian guidance equation.
- The equivariance condition and its assumptions.

Specify the role of the foliation and determine whether the construction is genuinely relativistically covariant or requires additional structure.

Do not assume that particle-based Bohmian equations can be transferred directly to quantum field configurations.

Account for any ultraviolet regularization, functional-derivative subtleties or renormalization requirements that affect the derivation.

### C. Derive the proposed gravitational source

Investigate what should actually gravitate.

Start with the stress-energy tensor derived from the original matter action.

Determine how the Bohmian configuration and quantum potential affect the effective source.

Investigate at least these possibilities:

1. The classical stress-energy evaluated on the actual field configuration.
2. An effective stress-energy containing the quantum-potential contribution.
3. A source derived through an appropriately justified effective action or Hamiltonian construction.

Do not assume these constructions are equivalent.

For each candidate, establish:

- Its mathematical definition.
- Whether it follows from the chosen dynamics.
- Whether it is covariant.
- Whether it depends explicitly on the foliation.
- Whether it satisfies the gravitational conservation requirement.

You may reject all three if none is mathematically defensible.

**Critical restriction:** Do not introduce an arbitrary correction term merely to force the source divergence to vanish. Any additional term must be independently derived from the specified dynamics or justified by an explicitly declared physical assumption.

### D. Conservation and gravitational consistency

Investigate:

\[
\nabla^\mu S_{\mu\nu}=0.
\]

Use the contracted Bianchi identity to establish what the gravitational equation requires.

Determine whether the conservation condition:

- Follows directly from the Bohmian guidance dynamics.
- Follows only after including quantum-potential terms.
- Requires exchange terms between quantum matter and gravitational geometry.
- Holds only for a restricted class of states or configurations.
- Requires a preferred foliation or additional physical structure.
- Fails for a nontrivial class of configurations.

Where relevant, derive the ADM Hamiltonian and momentum constraints and investigate their preservation.

Do not simply state that the field equations are consistent. Show the relevant mathematical relationship.

### E. Measurement and two-branch toy model

Investigate the smallest measurement model needed to expose the gravitational-source question.

Use a system-plus-apparatus model with two separated wavefunction branches:

\[
\Psi
=
c_1\Psi_1+c_2\Psi_2.
\]

Specify the actual Bohmian configuration and identify its occupied branch.

Compare:

- Expectation-value stress-energy sourcing.
- Actual-configuration sourcing.
- Any derived quantum-potential contribution.

Determine what the unoccupied branch contributes to the gravitational source and through which mathematical mechanism.

Do not assume that an empty branch contributes either zero or a nonzero gravitational field.

Derive the answer for the specified model.

Investigate whether the gravitational source remains consistent throughout the measurement interaction, including the formation of effectively separated branches.

### F. Independent obstruction search

Actively search for reasons the construction might fail.

At minimum investigate:

- Covariant conservation.
- Bianchi compatibility.
- Dependence on the preferred foliation.
- Relativistic consistency.
- Equivariance.
- Quantum equilibrium.
- Gravitational constraint preservation.
- Backreaction consistency.
- Classical and appropriate weak-coupling limits.

Identify the strongest known objections in the primary literature.

Do not dismiss an objection merely because an alternative interpretation appears attractive.
